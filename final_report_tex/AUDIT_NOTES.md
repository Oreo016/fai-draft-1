The proposal's Dec-POMDP language frames the problem. An exact Dec-POMDP solver, learned communication policies, multi-sender coordination, networking costs, and formal optimality claims are outside the implemented primary design. Paired common randomness reduces variance in some settings. It does not make finite, approximate value estimates unbiased. Oracle labels are themselves Monte Carlo estimates with the stated rollout budget.

## Independent analysis and denominator checks

Regenerate the primary and fresh secondary figures and tables with the commands in the report README. The validation uses JSON rows rather than published graphics, groups condition episodes by the same seeds, and keeps all context observations from a seed together in bootstrap resampling. The H1 estimand is the proportion of eligible contexts with a difference above 0.5 for the first non-intention report. H3 compares exact choices, including abstention, with delays determined by at least one pair's absolute oracle gain change greater than 0.5 from horizon 1 to 15. The H4 denominator is the mean of within-seed positive-send rates at zero cost and the bootstrap recomputes the same ratio of means. A zero low-cost rate makes a resampled relative effect undefined. The primary run has no such draws in the independent analysis. Pair regret uses the oracle best value including abstention. Receiver regret compares receivers for the message actually chosen and is conditional on sending. Primary VoC MAE first averages errors over shortlisted pairs in each context, then averages over contexts. Recall averages only across contexts containing at least one oracle pair above 0.5. The useful-message fraction is conditional on the model choosing to send and requires a positive oracle gain.

Use `python -B final_report_tex/scripts/check_legacy.py` for the separately validated nested [legacy results](data_summary/legacy_checks.json). The script reads the two H1 per-seed count files, every archived H2 episode list, and the H4 episode list. It uses 10,000 percentile bootstrap draws from seed clusters.

- Nested H1 focused run `h1_evaluation_20261121_230305_562535` examines the first non-intention report at each decision and accumulates up to ten eligible contexts per seed. The 838 observed decisions yield 500 eligible and 156 hits in seeds 610–659, 31.2% (95% CI 20.6–42.6%). The earlier `225928_494352` samples ten decision contexts per seed regardless of eligibility and obtains 66/232. Overlapping seeds and different stopping rules prevent pooling. The eligibility-conditioned legacy CI crosses the 30% target even though the point estimate exceeds it. Detailed oracle rollout pairs for these archived H1 counts were unavailable.
- Nested whole-horizon H2 quota-five cohorts `223852_784456` and `231120_101460` use identical environment and communication sections, equal saved scheduling descriptions, and disjoint seeds 510–559 and 710–759. Each policy can send at positive value and forces remaining sends as necessary at the episode deadline. All 100 seed pairs meet the quota. The pooled return difference is +0.5110 (95% CI +0.2886 to +0.7711). An earlier forced evenly spaced schedule `214955_584240` yields −0.3795 (95% CI −0.7905 to −0.0174) in 50 different seeds. These older runs demonstrate protocol-dependent observations. The contrast also changes seed block and does not estimate an isolated scheduling treatment effect. The nested quota-ten adaptive runs have quota misses and exclude them from exact-budget estimates. The nested Aware–Full “matched return” statistic selects pairs by their observed return differences and computes average per-seed communication reduction. It is not the interval-based return equivalence and pooled communication ratio used in the primary analysis.
- Nested H4 uses the aware H=5 policy over 50 matched seeds and four costs, 200 episodes. At 0 and 0.5 the mean per-seed positive-send rates are 15.16% and 2.87%, a relative reduction of 81.05% (ratio bootstrap 95% CI 74.75–85.66%). The saved archive CI instead summarizes per-seed relative reductions among positive denominators. The paper uses the independently computed ratio interval.
